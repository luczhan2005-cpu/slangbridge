1 · Realistic student messages

Each test case is translated by the baseline (MyMemory machine translation) and by SlangBridge (Same Vibe mode). An LLM judge scores both against the team's reference meaning with fixed anchors: meaning 0–2, slang & tone 0–2, naturalness 0–2 (total /6). You can override any score by hand.

2 · CIBuzzBench benchmark (unseen slang)

Random entries from the test split of CIBuzzBench (non-offensive). Each Chinese term is rendered into English by three systems; the judge checks it against the dataset's gold meaning (1 / 0.5 / 0). Test entries are excluded from retrieval, so the glossary cannot leak the answer.

Why SlangBridge?

Target users: international and exchange students who text across French, English and Chinese on WeChat and WhatsApp.

Problem: translation tools translate words, not intent. yyds becomes “forever god”, 我真的会谢 becomes “I will really thank”, j'ai le seum is left untranslated. Tone, sarcasm and cultural references disappear, and so does the friendship vibe.

Solution: an AI translator grounded in a slang database that renders slang as equivalent slang (Same Vibe), as plain language (Neutral), or with a full breakdown (Explain), adapted to who you are writing to.

Data sources

  • CIBuzzBench: 3,001 Chinese internet buzzwords with English meanings and equivalents (used for retrieval and for the benchmark).
  • Team-curated list of ZH / EN / FR slang with cross-language equivalents, built from: Wikipedia: Chinese Internet slang, Migaku, HSK Lord, LingoAce, Merriam-Webster slang, Wiktionnaire, Superprof, and our own experience in China.
  • Baseline: MyMemory free machine translation API.

Privacy & limits

  • Messages are sent to the AI provider and to the baseline MT service to be translated. The app stores nothing on its server. Remove names and personal details before pasting.
  • Slang changes fast: a term can be new, regional, or mean something else in a given friend group. Check the confidence level and the warnings.
  • The automatic judge is an LLM and can be biased toward its own outputs; the team reviews the scores by hand.
